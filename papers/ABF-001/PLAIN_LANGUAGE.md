# ABF-001 in plain language

ABF-001 studies a completely specified finite map from 16 input bits to 8 output bits and asks what happens to its algebraic complexity when the inputs satisfy one affine linear equation.

There are 131,070 affine hyperplanes. Rather than recomputing every restricted algebraic normal form, the paper uses XOR moments. The same moment equations can be read in two directions: fix a hyperplane and find output masks that lose degree, or fix an output mask and find hyperplanes causing the loss. One direction is an orthogonal complement; the other is a matrix kernel.

Exact results:

- 130,559 hyperplanes give vectorial degree 15;
- 511 give degree 14;
- none give degree 13 or lower;
- the 255 order-one symmetric matrices have ranks 13–17 with multiplicities 2, 15, 74, 112, and 52;
- 203 output masks have nonzero radicals, representing 202 distinct radical subspaces;
- the resulting incidence graph has 469 edges and 201 components.

The distinction between 203 mask-indexed radicals and 202 distinct subspaces is deliberate: masks `8a` and `9b` share one radical line generated by parameter `119d5`.

This is not a full hash break, peer review, external reproduction, formal verification, or proof of worldwide priority. It is a candidate Boolean-functions and finite-geometry contribution released for technical review.
