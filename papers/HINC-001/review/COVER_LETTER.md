**Manuscript:** *Hidden infinitesimal noncommutativity in endomorphism monoids of two graded Gerstenhaber algebras*

Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript for publication in **[Journal Name]**.

The paper classifies the complete affine monoid schemes of graded strict Gerstenhaber endomorphisms of two explicit characteristic-two Gerstenhaber algebras. The main structural result is that both monoids arise from one reducible crossing monoid in two different ways: an infinitesimal bilateral skew thickening in the even case and a discrete clopen unit-doubling construction in the odd case.

The most distinctive consequence is scheme-theoretic. Over every reduced base algebra, the even unit group appears commutative; over every field extension, all of its points appear central. Nevertheless, the full group scheme has trivial center, derived subgroup `alpha_2`, and abelianization `G_m`. Thus reduced points give the wrong answer to the universal centrality question.

The manuscript is self-contained. It defines the two Gerstenhaber algebras explicitly as subalgebras of the Hochschild cohomology of the dual numbers and proves the endomorphism classifications by direct coefficient calculations over arbitrary commutative characteristic-two base algebras. The paper also determines the common crossing core, connected components, unit loci, idempotent schemes, normalization, tangent dimensions, finite-field point counts, and zeta functions.

The surrounding theories of algebraic monoids, affine group schemes, Hochschild cohomology, and Gerstenhaber brackets are established. We do not claim invention of those mechanisms. The proposed contribution is the explicit pair, the common-core theorem, and the reduced-point-invisible noncommutativity and center phenomenon.

The calculations have been challenged by several independent implementations, including direct finite-Artin enumeration, a separately structured coefficient solver, exact Groebner-ideal checks, and a new standalone common-core verifier. These computations support but do not replace the all-base proofs.
