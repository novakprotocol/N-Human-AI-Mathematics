# Hidden infinitesimal noncommutativity in endomorphism monoids of two graded Gerstenhaber algebras

## Abstract

Let `F` be a field of characteristic two and put

\[
D=F[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2),
\qquad
HH^\bullet(D)\cong D[u],\quad |u|=1,
\]

with bracket

\[
[xu^i,yu^j]
=
\bigl(i\,x\partial(y)+j\,y\partial(x)\bigr)u^{i+j-1},
\qquad \partial(\epsilon)=1.
\]

Inside this Gerstenhaber algebra consider

\[
E=F+uD[u]\cong F[r,s]/(s^2),
\qquad r=u,\ s=\epsilon u,
\]

and

\[
O=D+F\epsilon u+u^2D[u].
\]

We classify the complete affine monoid schemes of graded strict Gerstenhaber endomorphisms of `E` and `O` over arbitrary commutative `F`-algebras.

For `E`, every endomorphism is uniquely

\[
r\mapsto\lambda r,
\qquad
s\mapsto\eta r+\delta s,
\qquad
\eta^2=0,
\qquad
\lambda(\delta-1)=0.
\]

The representing monoid is connected, nonreduced, and noncommutative. Its reduction is the commutative crossing

\[
C=\operatorname{Spec}F[x,y]/(x(y-1)).
\]

For `O`, every endomorphism is diagonal and the monoid is reduced and commutative. A group-like idempotent splits it as

\[
M_O\cong \mathbf G_m\sqcup C.
\]

The two cases are therefore modifications of one common core:

\[
M_E\cong C\times\alpha_2
\]

as a scheme, with bilateral skew multiplication

\[
(x,y,e)(x',y',e')=(xx',yy',xe'+y'e),
\]

whereas `M_O` is obtained by adjoining a new clopen copy of the unit group of `C`.

The scheme-theoretic center of `M_E` consists only of zero and identity. Its unit group is `\alpha_2\rtimes\mathbf G_m`, has trivial center, derived subgroup `\alpha_2`, and abelianization `\mathbf G_m`. Nevertheless, over every reduced base algebra the infinitesimal coordinate vanishes, so all ordinary field-valued unit groups appear abelian and central. This is the central phenomenon: reduced points give the wrong answer to a universal centrality question.

We also determine idempotent schemes, connected components, normalization of the common core, tangent dimensions, finite-field point counts, and zeta functions. A new standalone verifier, independent of the earlier project implementations, checks the common-core laws over `F_2[t]/(t^n)` for `1<=n<=4` and performs exact symbolic identities; all recorded discrepancies are zero.

The paper is self-contained as a theorem about the explicit Gerstenhaber algebras `E` and `O`. Their occurrence as even and odd singular-corner images in the Modular Carry-Rees Collapse family is an application, not a logical prerequisite.

## 1. The two explicit Gerstenhaber algebras

Let `F` be a field of characteristic two, let

\[
D=F[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2),
\]

and let `\partial:D\to F` be the derivation determined by `\partial(\epsilon)=1`. The normalized Hochschild cochain calculation for the dual numbers gives

\[
HH^\bullet(D)\cong D[u],\qquad |u|=1,
\]

with ordinary multiplication and Gerstenhaber bracket

\[
[xu^i,yu^j]
=
\bigl(i\,x\partial(y)+j\,y\partial(x)\bigr)u^{i+j-1}.
\]

Define the graded Gerstenhaber subalgebras

\[
E=F+uD[u]
\]

and

\[
O=D+F\epsilon u+u^2D[u].
\]

Set

\[
r=u,\qquad s=\epsilon u.
\]

Then

\[
E\cong F[r,s]/(s^2),
\qquad |r|=|s|=1,
\qquad [r,s]=r.
\]

For `O`, set

\[
a=\epsilon u,\qquad b=u^2,\qquad c=u^3.
\]

Then

\[
O\cong
\frac{F[\epsilon,a,b,c]}
{(\epsilon^2,\epsilon a,a^2,ab-\epsilon c,
ac-\epsilon b^2,c^2-b^3)},
\]

where the degrees are `0,1,2,3`, and representative brackets are

\[
[a,\epsilon]=\epsilon,
\qquad[c,\epsilon]=b,
\qquad[a,c]=c,
\qquad[a,b]=[b,c]=0.
\]

All relations and brackets follow directly from the embedding in `D[u]`. Thus the objects classified below are explicit and require no appeal to the MCRC presentation.

### Application to the MCRC parity family

The project-level singular-corner theorem identifies `E` with the even-modulus strict restriction image and `O` with the odd-modulus image. Under that identification, every theorem below becomes a parity invariant of the MCRC family. The endomorphism-monoid classification itself, however, is a standalone statement about `E` and `O`.

## 2. Functors of points and representability

For a commutative `F`-algebra `R`, write

\[
E_R=E\otimes_F R,
\qquad
O_R=O\otimes_F R.
\]

A graded strict Gerstenhaber endomorphism is an `R`-linear, unital, degree-preserving map preserving the cup product and bracket. Define

\[
\mathcal E(R)=\operatorname{End}^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{Gerst}}(E_R),
\qquad
\mathcal O(R)=\operatorname{End}^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{Gerst}}(O_R).
\]

Because `E` and `O` are finitely generated by homogeneous elements with finitely many algebra and bracket relations, these functors are represented by affine schemes cut out by coefficient equations. Composition is polynomial in the coefficients, so the representing schemes are affine monoid schemes.

Composition is written from right to left: `xy` means first `y`, then `x`.

## 3. The even endomorphism monoid

The degree-one part of `E_R` is free on `r,s`.  A general graded `R`-linear map is therefore

\[
\phi(r)=\lambda r+\beta s,
\qquad
\phi(s)=\eta r+\delta s.
\]

### Lemma 3.1 -- the algebra relation

Preservation of `s^2=0` is equivalent to

\[
\eta^2=0.
\]

#### Proof

Characteristic two eliminates the mixed term:

\[
\phi(s)^2
=(\eta r+\delta s)^2
=\eta^2r^2.
\]

The monomial `r^2` spans a free `R`-summand of `E_R`, so its coefficient must vanish. QED.

### Lemma 3.2 -- bracket elimination

Preservation of `[r,s]=r` is equivalent to

\[
\beta=0,
\qquad
\lambda(\delta-1)=0.
\]

#### Proof

In characteristic two, `[s,r]=[r,s]=r`.  Hence

\[
[\phi(r),\phi(s)]
=(\lambda\delta+\beta\eta)r.
\]

This must equal

\[
\phi([r,s])=\lambda r+\beta s.
\]

Comparison of the free coefficients gives `\beta=0`, followed by
`\lambda\delta=\lambda`. QED.

### Theorem 3.3 -- complete even endomorphism functor

For every commutative `F`-algebra `R`,

\[
\mathcal E(R)
=
\left\{
(\lambda,\delta,\eta)\in R^3:
\eta^2=0,
\ \lambda(\delta-1)=0
\right\},
\]

where

\[
r\mapsto\lambda r,
\qquad
s\mapsto\eta r+\delta s.
\]

Composition is

\[
(\lambda,\delta,\eta)
(\lambda',\delta',\eta')
=
(\lambda\lambda',\delta\delta',
\lambda\eta'+\delta'\eta).
\]

#### Proof

Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2 prove necessity.  Conversely, the displayed relations preserve `s^2=0` and `[r,s]=r`; the remaining generator brackets vanish before and after applying the map.  Direct substitution gives the composition formula, and the defining equations are stable under that formula. QED.

### Corollary 3.4 -- coordinate bialgebra

The functor `\mathcal E` is represented by the affine monoid scheme

\[
M_E=\operatorname{Spec}A_E,
\qquad
A_E=
F[\lambda,\delta,\eta]/(\eta^2,\lambda(\delta-1)),
\]

with

\[
\Delta(\lambda)=\lambda\otimes\lambda,
\qquad
\Delta(\delta)=\delta\otimes\delta,
\]

\[
\Delta(\eta)
=\lambda\otimes\eta+\eta\otimes\delta,
\]

and counit

\[
\varepsilon(\lambda)=\varepsilon(\delta)=1,
\qquad
\varepsilon(\eta)=0.
\]

### Proposition 3.5 -- geometry and multiplication phase

The monoid scheme `M_E` has the following properties.

1. It is one-dimensional and connected.
2. It is nonreduced, with nilpotent parameter `\eta`.
3. It is noncommutative as a monoid scheme.
4. Its reduction is the commutative monoid

   \[
   (M_E)_{\mathrm{red}}
   =\operatorname{Spec}
   F[\lambda,\delta]/(\lambda(\delta-1)),
   \]

   the union of two affine lines crossing at `(0,1)`.

#### Proof

The nilpotent ideal `(\eta)` does not change the underlying topological space.  The reduced equation factors as

\[
\lambda(\delta-1)=0,
\]

so the reduction is two affine lines with nonempty intersection and is therefore connected and one-dimensional.

When `\eta=0`, composition is coordinatewise in `\lambda,\delta`, hence commutative.  The full monoid is not commutative.  Over the dual numbers

\[
R=F[\tau]/(\tau^2),
\]

consider

\[
x=(1,1,\tau),
\qquad
y=(0,1,0).
\]

Then

\[
xy=(0,1,\tau),
\qquad
yx=(0,1,0).
\]

Thus the multiplication morphism is not invariant under transposition. QED.

### Proposition 3.6 -- units and their closure

The unit group of `M_E` is

\[
G_E=D(\lambda)
\cong\alpha_2\rtimes\mathbf G_m,
\]

with

\[
\delta=1,
\qquad
\lambda\in R^\times,
\qquad
\eta^2=0.
\]

It is open but not closed.  Its scheme-theoretic closure is the component

\[
V(\delta-1)
=
\operatorname{Spec}F[\lambda,\eta]/(\eta^2),
\]

which contains the nonunits with `\lambda=0`.

#### Proof

The degree-one matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda&\eta\\
0&\delta
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It is invertible exactly when `\lambda\delta` is a unit.  The relation
`\lambda(\delta-1)=0` then forces `\delta=1`, so the unit locus is `D(\lambda)`.  Localizing `A_E` at `\lambda` kills `\delta-1` and gives the stated group scheme.  The saturation of the zero ideal by powers of `\lambda` is `(\delta-1)`, so its closure is `V(\delta-1)`, which contains the point `\lambda=0`. QED.
