# Hidden infinitesimal noncommutativity in two binary-Gerstenhaber endomorphism monoid schemes

**Author:** Matthew S. Novak<br>
**Paper ID:** HINC-001<br>
**Release status:** candidate manuscript prepared for public technical review<br>
**External specialist review:** pending<br>
**Historical priority:** unestablished

## Abstract

Let `F` be a field of characteristic two and let

\[
D=F[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2).
\]

A direct normalized-cochain calculation gives

\[
HH^\bullet(D,D)\cong D[u],\qquad |u|=1,
\]

with binary Gerstenhaber bracket

\[
[xu^i,yu^j]
=
\bigl(i\,x\partial(y)+j\,y\partial(x)\bigr)u^{i+j-1},
\qquad \partial(\epsilon)=1.
\]

Inside this graded algebra consider

\[
E=F+uD[u]
\quad\text{and}\quad
O=D+F\epsilon u+u^2D[u].
\]

We give a self-contained candidate classification, over every commutative `F`-algebra `R`, of the unital, `R`-linear, degree-preserving maps that preserve the cup product and the displayed binary bracket.

For `E`, every such endomorphism is uniquely

\[
r\mapsto \lambda r,
\qquad
s\mapsto \eta r+\delta s,
\qquad
\eta^2=0,
\qquad
\lambda(\delta-1)=0,
\]

where `r=u` and `s=\epsilon u`. Composition is

\[
(\lambda,\delta,\eta)(\lambda',\delta',\eta')
=
(\lambda\lambda',\delta\delta',\lambda\eta'+\delta'\eta).
\]

For `O`, with

\[
a=\epsilon u,\qquad b=u^2,\qquad c=u^3,
\]

every endomorphism is uniquely diagonal,

\[
\epsilon\mapsto\lambda\epsilon,
\quad a\mapsto\mu a,
\quad b\mapsto p b,
\quad c\mapsto\rho c,
\]

subject to

\[
p=\lambda\rho,
\qquad
\rho=\lambda p^2,
\qquad
\lambda(\mu-1)=0,
\qquad
\rho(\mu-1)=0.
\]

The resulting affine monoid schemes share the reduced crossing

\[
C=\operatorname{Spec}F[x,y]/(x(y-1)).
\]

The even scheme is `C\times\alpha_2` **as a scheme**, with constrained upper-triangular multiplication

\[
(x,y,e)(x',y',e')=(xx',yy',xe'+y'e).
\]

The odd scheme is reduced and commutative; a group-like idempotent separates a torus unit component from a clopen crossing ideal. We distinguish the scheme-theoretic center from pointwise centers of individual `R`-point monoids and prove that

\[
Z_{\mathrm{sch}}(M_E)
\cong
\operatorname{Spec}F[z]/(z(z-1)).
\]

Thus its `R`-points are the idempotents of `R`; on connected bases they are zero and identity, while pointwise centers of `M_E(R)` can be larger. The even unit group is `\alpha_2\rtimes\mathbf G_m`; its commutator formula is explicit, and its fppf-derived subgroup is `\alpha_2` under the convention fixed below.

The coefficient classifications and normalized monoid laws have bounded public Lean verification. The ambient Hochschild calculation, odd presentation theorem, generator-to-global lemma, representability argument, complete center calculation, clopen split, and fppf-derived statement are proved here on paper but are not all formalized. No exact indexed occurrence of the complete paired classification was located; worldwide priority is not claimed.

## 1. Conventions and claim scope

### 1.1 Binary Gerstenhaber endomorphisms

In this paper a **binary Gerstenhaber algebra** means a graded-commutative unital algebra equipped with a degree-minus-one graded Lie bracket satisfying the biderivation identities.

A **binary Gerstenhaber endomorphism** is a map that is:

1. unital;
2. linear over the declared base algebra;
3. degree-preserving;
4. cup-product-preserving;
5. preserving the binary degree-minus-one bracket.

No preservation of a Batalin–Vilkovisky operator, restricted power, Gerstenhaber square beyond the binary bracket, brace algebra, or full `E_2` structure is claimed.

### 1.2 Base change and point terminology

All classification statements are functorial in a commutative `F`-algebra `R`. We distinguish:

- `F`-rational points;
- points over field extensions of `F`;
- geometric points;
- points over reduced `F`-algebras;
- points over arbitrary `F`-algebras.

The informal phrase “ordinary points” is used only after the relevant exact category is named.

### 1.3 Centers and derived subgroups

For an affine monoid scheme `M`, the **scheme-theoretic center** is the closed subfunctor

\[
Z_{\mathrm{sch}}(M)(R)
=
\{z\in M(R):
\text{for every }R\to S\text{ and every }m\in M(S),
 z_Sm=mz_S\}.
\]

This must not be confused with the center of the single abstract monoid `M(R)`, which may be larger.

For an affine group scheme `G`, `[G,G]_{\mathrm{fppf}}` denotes the fppf sheaf generated by the commutator morphism, equivalently the smallest normal fppf subgroup sheaf containing its image. The abelianization is the corresponding fppf quotient.

### 1.4 Composition convention

Composition is written from right to left: `xy` means first `y`, then `x`.

## 2. The characteristic-two Hochschild foundation

Let

\[
D=F1\oplus F\epsilon,\qquad \epsilon^2=0,
\]

and let `\bar D=D/F1=F\epsilon`.

### Proposition 2.1 — normalized cochains and vanishing differential

For every `n\ge0`, evaluation on `\epsilon^{\otimes n}` identifies

\[
C^n(D,D)=\operatorname{Hom}_F(\bar D^{\otimes n},D)
\]

with `D`. Let `c_{n,x}` denote the cochain satisfying

\[
c_{n,x}(\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon)=x.
\]

The normalized Hochschild differential vanishes identically.

#### Proof

Every interior differential term contains `\epsilon^2` and is zero. The two boundary terms are

\[
\epsilon x+x\epsilon=2\epsilon x=0
\]

because `D` is commutative and `\operatorname{char}F=2`. Therefore every cochain is a cocycle and there are no nonzero coboundaries. ∎

### Proposition 2.2 — cup product

Let `u=[c_{1,1}]`. Then

\[
HH^\bullet(D,D)\cong D[u],
\qquad |\epsilon|=0,\quad |u|=1.
\]

#### Proof

For normalized cochains,

\[
(c_{i,x}\smile c_{j,y})(\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon)=xy.
\]

Hence `[c_{n,x}]=xu^n`, and the cup product is ordinary multiplication in `D[u]`. In characteristic two, graded commutativity does not force `u^2` to vanish. ∎

### Proposition 2.3 — binary Gerstenhaber bracket

Let `\partial:D\to F` be the derivation determined by `\partial(\epsilon)=1`. Then

\[
[xu^i,yu^j]
=
\bigl(i\,x\partial(y)+j\,y\partial(x)\bigr)u^{i+j-1}.
\]

The integers `i` and `j` act through their parity in `F`.

#### Proof

When the output `y=y_0+y_1\epsilon` of `c_{j,y}` is inserted into a normalized cochain, only the `\epsilon` coefficient survives. Each of the `i` insertion positions contributes `x\partial(y)`. All insertion signs become `+1` in characteristic two, so

\[
c_{i,x}\circ c_{j,y}
=i\,x\partial(y)c_{i+j-1,1}.
\]

Interchanging the cochains and taking the graded commutator gives the formula. ∎

## 3. The two explicit binary-Gerstenhaber algebras

### 3.1 The even algebra

Set

\[
r=u,\qquad s=\epsilon u.
\]

Then

\[
E=F+uD[u]
\cong
F[r,s]/(s^2),
\qquad |r|=|s|=1,
\]

with generator brackets

\[
[r,s]=r,\qquad [r,r]=[s,s]=0.
\]

### 3.2 The odd algebra

Set

\[
a=\epsilon u,\qquad b=u^2,\qquad c=u^3.
\]

Define

\[
B=
\frac{F[\epsilon,a,b,c]}
{(\epsilon^2,\epsilon a,a^2,ab-\epsilon c,
ac-\epsilon b^2,c^2-b^3)}.
\]

### Proposition 3.1 — complete presentation of `O`

The map

\[
B\longrightarrow D[u],
\qquad
\epsilon\mapsto\epsilon,
\quad a\mapsto\epsilon u,
\quad b\mapsto u^2,
\quad c\mapsto u^3,
\]

is injective and has image

\[
O=D+F\epsilon u+u^2D[u].
\]

#### Proof

Using `c^2=b^3`, every polynomial in `b,c` reduces to

\[
f(b)+cg(b).
\]

Multiplication by `\epsilon` gives

\[
\epsilon h(b)+\epsilon c k(b).
\]

Words containing `a` reduce using

\[
\epsilon a=0,\qquad a^2=0,\qquad
ab^m=\epsilon c b^{m-1}\ (m\ge1),\qquad
acb^m=\epsilon b^{m+2}\ (m\ge0).
\]

Thus every element has a representative

\[
f(b)+cg(b)+\epsilon h(b)+\epsilon ck(b)+\tau a.
\]

Its image is a linear combination of the pairwise distinct basis elements

\[
u^{2m},\quad u^{2m+3},\quad
\epsilon u^{2m},\quad \epsilon u^{2m+3},\quad
\epsilon u.
\]

These are exactly the standard basis elements belonging to `O`, so the normal form is unique. ∎

The nonzero generator brackets needed below are

\[
[a,\epsilon]=\epsilon,\qquad
[c,\epsilon]=b,\qquad
[a,c]=c,
\]

while

\[
[a,b]=[b,c]=0.
\]

The remaining generator brackets follow from the formula in Proposition 2.3.

## 4. Extension from generators and representability

### Lemma 4.1 — generator-to-global bracket preservation

Let `A` be a binary Gerstenhaber algebra generated as a unital algebra by homogeneous elements `g_1,\ldots,g_m`. Let `B` be another binary Gerstenhaber algebra, and let

\[
\phi:A\to B
\]

be a unital graded algebra homomorphism. If

\[
\phi([g_i,g_j])=[\phi(g_i),\phi(g_j)]
\]

for every pair of generators, then `\phi` preserves the bracket on all of `A`.

#### Proof

The bracket is a graded derivation in each variable. For homogeneous elements,

\[
[x,yz]=[x,y]z+(-1)^{(|x|-1)|y|}y[x,z]
\]

and

\[
[xy,z]=x[y,z]+(-1)^{|y|(|z|-1)}[x,z]y.
\]

Use induction on the total word length of a pair of monomials. The generator-pair case is the hypothesis. If either monomial is a product, apply the corresponding biderivation identity, the induction hypothesis, and multiplicativity of `\phi`. Extend by linearity. In characteristic two the signs specialize to `+1`, but the argument is valid before specialization. ∎

### Proposition 4.2 — explicit representability method

The even and odd binary-Gerstenhaber endomorphism functors are represented by affine monoid schemes.

#### Proof

For each source algebra, choose the finite homogeneous generating set displayed in Section 3. Because maps are degree-preserving, each generator image lies in a finite free homogeneous component. Write the most general image using finitely many coefficient variables.

The requirements that the map be unital, satisfy every defining algebra relation, and preserve every generator-pair bracket are polynomial equations in those coefficients. Propositions 3.1 and 4.1 show that solutions are naturally equivalent to global binary-Gerstenhaber endomorphisms. Therefore the functor is represented by the closed subscheme of the finite coefficient affine space cut out by those equations.

Substitution of generator images expresses composition polynomially. The identity assignment gives a rational point. Dually, polynomial composition and identity define the comultiplication and counit of the coordinate bialgebra. The construction is natural under every base change `R\to S`. ∎

## 5. The even endomorphism monoid

Let `R` be a commutative `F`-algebra. A general graded map on degree one is

\[
\phi(r)=\lambda r+\beta s,
\qquad
\phi(s)=\eta r+\delta s.
\]

### Lemma 5.1 — algebra relation

Preservation of `s^2=0` is equivalent to

\[
\eta^2=0.
\]

#### Proof

Characteristic two eliminates the mixed term:

\[
(\eta r+\delta s)^2=\eta^2r^2.
\]

The monomial `r^2` spans a free `R`-summand, so its coefficient must vanish. ∎

### Lemma 5.2 — bracket equations

Preservation of `[r,s]=r` is equivalent to

\[
\beta=0,\qquad \lambda(\delta-1)=0.
\]

#### Proof

In characteristic two, `[s,r]=[r,s]=r`. Hence

\[
[\phi(r),\phi(s)]
=(\lambda\delta+\beta\eta)r.
\]

This must equal

\[
\phi([r,s])=\lambda r+\beta s.
\]

Comparing free coefficients gives `\beta=0` and then `\lambda\delta=\lambda`. ∎

### Theorem 5.3 — even binary-Gerstenhaber endomorphisms

For every commutative `F`-algebra `R`, the endomorphisms of `E_R` are exactly

\[
\mathcal E(R)=
\{(\lambda,\delta,\eta)\in R^3:
\eta^2=0,\ \lambda(\delta-1)=0\},
\]

acting by

\[
r\mapsto\lambda r,\qquad
s\mapsto\eta r+\delta s.
\]

Composition is

\[
(\lambda,\delta,\eta)(\lambda',\delta',\eta')
=
(\lambda\lambda',\delta\delta',
\lambda\eta'+\delta'\eta).
\]

#### Proof

Lemmas 5.1 and 5.2 prove necessity. Conversely, the displayed equations define a graded algebra endomorphism preserving the complete generator bracket table. Lemma 4.1 gives global bracket preservation. Direct substitution gives the composition formula, and the defining equations are stable under it. ∎

### Corollary 5.4 — coordinate bialgebra

The representing affine monoid scheme is

\[
M_E=\operatorname{Spec}A_E,
\qquad
A_E=F[\lambda,\delta,\eta]/(\eta^2,\lambda(\delta-1)),
\]

with

\[
\Delta(\lambda)=\lambda\otimes\lambda,
\qquad
\Delta(\delta)=\delta\otimes\delta,
\]

\[
\Delta(\eta)=\lambda\otimes\eta+\eta\otimes\delta,
\]

and counit

\[
\varepsilon(\lambda)=\varepsilon(\delta)=1,
\qquad
\varepsilon(\eta)=0.
\]

### Proposition 5.5 — geometry and units

The scheme `M_E` is one-dimensional, connected, and nonreduced. Its reduction is

\[
C=\operatorname{Spec}F[\lambda,\delta]/(\lambda(\delta-1)),
\]

the union of two affine lines meeting at `(0,1)`. The multiplication is noncommutative.

The unit group is the open subscheme

\[
G_E=D(\lambda),
\]

on which `\delta=1`. It is isomorphic, as a group scheme, to

\[
\alpha_2\rtimes\mathbf G_m.
\]

#### Proof

The nilpotent ideal `(\eta)` does not change the underlying topological space. The reduced equation factors as `\lambda(\delta-1)=0`, giving the connected crossing. Noncommutativity is witnessed over `R=F[\tau]/(\tau^2)` by

\[
x=(1,1,\tau),\qquad y=(0,1,0),
\]

for which

\[
xy=(0,1,\tau),\qquad yx=(0,1,0).
\]

The degree-one matrix is upper triangular,

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda&\eta\\
0&\delta
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It is invertible exactly when `\lambda\delta` is a unit. The relation forces `\delta=1`, giving the stated unit group. ∎

## 6. The odd endomorphism monoid

The relevant homogeneous pieces are

\[
O_0=R1\oplus R\epsilon,\quad
O_1=Ra,\quad
O_2=Rb\oplus R\epsilon b,\quad
O_3=Rc\oplus R\epsilon c.
\]

A general unital graded map begins as

\[
\phi(\epsilon)=x+\lambda\epsilon,
\quad
\phi(a)=\mu a,
\quad
\phi(b)=pb+q\epsilon b,
\quad
\phi(c)=\rho c+\sigma\epsilon c.
\]

### Lemma 6.1 — bracket equations

Preserving the generator bracket table gives

\[
x=0,\qquad q=0,\qquad \sigma=0,
\]

\[
p=\lambda\rho,\qquad
\lambda(\mu-1)=0,\qquad
\rho(\mu-1)=0.
\]

#### Proof

From `[a,\epsilon]=\epsilon`,

\[
[\mu a,x+\lambda\epsilon]
=\mu\lambda\epsilon
=x+\lambda\epsilon,
\]

so `x=0` and `\lambda(\mu-1)=0`.

From `[a,c]=c`,

\[
[\mu a,\rho c+\sigma\epsilon c]
=\mu\rho c
=\rho c+\sigma\epsilon c,
\]

so `\rho(\mu-1)=0` and `\sigma=0`.

Finally `[c,\epsilon]=b` gives

\[
[\rho c,\lambda\epsilon]
=\lambda\rho b
=pb+q\epsilon b,
\]

so `p=\lambda\rho` and `q=0`. ∎

### Lemma 6.2 — algebra equations

After Lemma 6.1, preservation of the defining algebra relations is equivalent to

\[
\rho=\lambda p^2.
\]

#### Proof

The relation `ac=\epsilon b^2` becomes

\[
\mu\rho=\lambda p^2.
\]

Since `\rho(\mu-1)=0`, this is exactly `\rho=\lambda p^2`.

The relation `ab=\epsilon c` becomes `\mu p=\lambda\rho`, which follows from `p=\lambda\rho` and `\lambda(\mu-1)=0`. The relation `c^2=b^3` follows by substituting `p=\lambda\rho` into `\rho=\lambda p^2`, obtaining `\rho=\lambda^3\rho^2`, and then multiplying by `\rho`:

\[
\rho^2=\lambda^3\rho^3=p^3.
\]

All remaining relations are automatic. ∎

### Theorem 6.3 — odd binary-Gerstenhaber endomorphisms

For every commutative `F`-algebra `R`, the endomorphisms of `O_R` are exactly the diagonal maps

\[
\epsilon\mapsto\lambda\epsilon,
\qquad
a\mapsto\mu a,
\qquad
b\mapsto p b,
\qquad
c\mapsto\rho c,
\]

such that

\[
p=\lambda\rho,\qquad
\rho=\lambda p^2,\qquad
\lambda(\mu-1)=0,\qquad
\rho(\mu-1)=0.
\]

Composition is coordinatewise.

#### Proof

Lemmas 6.1 and 6.2 prove necessity. Proposition 3.1 proves that the displayed relations are a complete presentation of `O`. Conversely, the equations preserve every algebra relation and generator-pair bracket. Lemma 4.1 gives global bracket preservation. Diagonality gives coordinatewise composition, and the equations are stable under it. ∎

### Corollary 6.4 — coordinate bialgebra

The representing affine monoid scheme is

\[
M_O=\operatorname{Spec}A_O,
\]

where

\[
A_O=
\frac{F[\lambda,\mu,p,\rho]}
{(p-\lambda\rho,
\rho-\lambda p^2,
\lambda(\mu-1),
\rho(\mu-1))}.
\]

All four coordinates are group-like:

\[
\Delta(t)=t\otimes t,\qquad
\varepsilon(t)=1
\quad
(t\in\{\lambda,\mu,p,\rho\}).
\]

Eliminating `p` gives

\[
A_O\cong
\frac{F[\lambda,\mu,\rho]}
{(\lambda(\mu-1),
\rho(\mu-1),
\rho(1-\lambda^3\rho))}.
\]

### Theorem 6.5 — clopen decomposition

The element

\[
z=\lambda^3\rho
\]

is a group-like idempotent. It gives a canonical decomposition of affine monoid schemes

\[
M_O\cong G\sqcup C,
\]

where

\[
G=V(z-1)\cong\mathbf G_m
\]

is the unit group and

\[
C=V(z)
\cong
\operatorname{Spec}F[\lambda,\mu]/(\lambda(\mu-1))
\]

is a clopen two-sided ideal.

#### Proof

The relation `\rho=\lambda^3\rho^2` gives

\[
z=\lambda^3\rho=\lambda^6\rho^2=z^2.
\]

Group-likeness follows from group-likeness of `\lambda` and `\rho`. An idempotent splits the coordinate ring:

\[
A_O\cong A_O/(z)\times A_O/(z-1).
\]

On `z=0`, the relation `\rho(1-z)=0` gives `\rho=0`, then `p=0`, leaving the crossing equation `\lambda(\mu-1)=0`.

On `z=1`, both `\lambda` and `\rho` are units, so `\mu=1`; setting `t=\lambda^{-1}` gives

\[
\lambda=t^{-1},\qquad p=t^2,\qquad \rho=t^3.
\]

Thus this component is `\mathbf G_m`. Because `z(xy)=z(x)z(y)`, the `z=0` component is a two-sided ideal. ∎

### Functor-of-points qualification

For a connected test algebra `R`, every `R`-point lies in exactly one clopen component. For arbitrary `R`, a point of the disjoint-union scheme may choose different components on complementary clopen subsets of `\operatorname{Spec}R`. Equivalently, a nontrivial idempotent of `R` can encode a mixed component choice. Therefore the scheme decomposition must not be paraphrased as the naive set identity

\[
M_O(R)=G(R)\sqcup C(R)
\]

for all disconnected `R`.

## 7. The common crossing and the paired modifications

Put

\[
C=\operatorname{Spec}A_C,
\qquad
A_C=F[x,y]/(x(y-1)),
\]

with coordinatewise multiplication

\[
(x,y)(x',y')=(xx',yy').
\]

The identity is `(1,1)`, and the unit group is the `\mathbf G_m` locus `D(x)` on the branch `y=1`.

### Theorem 7.1 — even constrained square-zero extension

There is an isomorphism of schemes

\[
M_E\cong C\times\alpha_2.
\]

It is not an isomorphism of monoid schemes. Under this scheme identification,

\[
(x,y,e)(x',y',e')
=(xx',yy',xe'+y'e).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
(x,y,e)\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}
x&e\\
0&y
\end{pmatrix}
\]

identifies the multiplication with ordinary upper-triangular matrix multiplication subject to

\[
x(y-1)=0,\qquad e^2=0.
\]

The commutativity defect is

\[
\Omega((x,y,e),(x',y',e'))
=(x+y)e'+(x'+y')e.
\]

### Theorem 7.2 — odd separate clopen unit component

The odd scheme consists of the crossing ideal `C` together with a separate clopen torus unit component. A torus point with parameter `t` acts on a crossing point by multiplying its first coordinate and leaving its second coordinate fixed.

The local phrase “unit doubling” may be used only after this standard idempotent-splitting mechanism is stated explicitly; it is not a novelty claim.

### Paired summary

\[
\boxed{\text{even: common crossing plus a constrained skew square-zero direction}}
\]

\[
\boxed{\text{odd: common crossing plus a separate clopen torus unit component}}
\]

## 8. The scheme-theoretic center and the even unit group

### Theorem 8.1 — center of the even monoid scheme

The scheme-theoretic center of `M_E` is represented by

\[
Z_{\mathrm{sch}}(M_E)
\cong
\operatorname{Spec}F[z]/(z(z-1)),
\]

embedded by

\[
z\longmapsto(z,z,0).
\]

Consequently,

\[
Z_{\mathrm{sch}}(M_E)(R)
=
\{(e,e,0):e^2=e\in R\}.
\]

For connected `R`, these are zero and identity. For disconnected `R`, they are the idempotent mixtures associated with clopen decompositions of `\operatorname{Spec}R`.

#### Proof

Let `(x,y,e)` be universally central. Commuting with the valid point `(0,1,0)` forces `e=0`. After base change to `S=R[t]/(t^2)`, commuting with `(0,0,t)` gives

\[
xt=yt.
\]

Because `S=R\oplus Rt` as an `R`-module, `x=y`. The crossing relation becomes

\[
x(x-1)=0.
\]

Conversely, if `e=0`, `x=y`, and `x^2=x`, the defect formula in Theorem 7.1 vanishes against every base-changed point. ∎

### Pointwise-center warning

The abstract center of `M_E(R)` can be strictly larger than `Z_{\mathrm{sch}}(M_E)(R)`. Scheme-theoretic centrality is a universal base-change condition; pointwise centrality in one monoid is not.

For example:

| test algebra `R` | `|M_E(R)|` | pointwise center of `M_E(R)` | `|Z_{\mathrm{sch}}(M_E)(R)|` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `F_2` | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| `F_2[t]/(t^2)` | 16 | 4 | 2 |
| `F_2\times F_2` | 9 | 9 | 4 |
| `F_2[u,v]/(u^2,v^2)` | 448 | 4 | 2 |

### Theorem 8.2 — unit-group law and commutator

Write points of `G_E` as `(a,c)` with `a\in R^\times` and `c^2=0`. Then

\[
(a,c)(b,d)=(ab,ad+c),
\]

\[
(a,c)^{-1}=(a^{-1},a^{-1}c),
\]

and

\[
[(a,c),(b,d)]
=
\bigl(1,(a+1)d+(b+1)c\bigr).
\]

The scheme-theoretic center of `G_E` is trivial.

#### Proof

The formulas follow by direct multiplication. A universally central `(a,c)` must commute after base change with every infinitesimal translation `(1,d)`, forcing `a=1`. After a further faithfully flat localization with a free unit parameter `b` and `b+1` invertible, commuting with `(b,0)` forces `c=0`. ∎

### Theorem 8.3 — fppf-derived subgroup and abelianization

Under the convention of Section 1.3,

\[
[G_E,G_E]_{\mathrm{fppf}}=\alpha_2,
\qquad
G_E^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong\mathbf G_m.
\]

#### Proof

The commutator formula shows that every commutator lies in the normal kernel `\alpha_2` of the projection

\[
\pi:G_E\to\mathbf G_m,\qquad (a,c)\mapsto a.
\]

Let `n\in\alpha_2(R)`. The algebra

\[
S=R[t,t^{-1},(t+1)^{-1}]
\]

is faithfully flat over `R`: it is a localization of a polynomial algebra, and every fiber over a prime of `R` is nonzero. In `G_E(S)`, set

\[
(a,c)=(t,0),\qquad
(b,d)=(1,(t+1)^{-1}n).
\]

Then

\[
[(a,c),(b,d)]=(1,n).
\]

Thus every section of `\alpha_2` is fppf-locally a commutator. The fppf sheaf generated by commutators is therefore exactly `\alpha_2`. The quotient by the kernel of `\pi` is `\mathbf G_m`, giving the abelianization. ∎

For every field extension `K/F`, `\alpha_2(K)=0`, so

\[
G_E(K)=K^\times.
\]

Thus field-extension point groups are abelian even though the group scheme has nontrivial infinitesimal commutators.

## 9. Idempotents, geometry, and arithmetic fingerprints

### Proposition 9.1 — idempotent schemes

The even idempotent scheme is

\[
\operatorname{Idem}(M_E)
\cong
\operatorname{Spec}F
\sqcup
\alpha_2
\sqcup
\operatorname{Spec}F.
\]

The components are `(0,0,0)`, `(0,1,\eta)` with `\eta^2=0`, and `(1,1,0)`.

The odd idempotent scheme is the disjoint union of four reduced points:

\[
(0,0,0,0),\quad
(0,1,0,0),\quad
(1,1,0,0),\quad
(1,1,1,1).
\]

#### Proof

For the even law, idempotence gives

\[
\lambda^2=\lambda,\qquad
\delta^2=\delta,\qquad
(\lambda+\delta-1)\eta=0,
\]

together with the defining equations. The four diagonal idempotent pairs reduce to the three displayed cases.

For the odd law, composition is coordinatewise. Requiring all four coordinates to be idempotent and imposing the defining equations yields exactly the four displayed points. ∎

### Proposition 9.2 — normalization and tangent dimensions

The normalization of the crossing is

\[
\widetilde C\cong\mathbf A^1\sqcup\mathbf A^1.
\]

The normalization of the reduced odd monoid is

\[
\mathbf G_m\sqcup\mathbf A^1\sqcup\mathbf A^1.
\]

At the crossing, `\dim T C=2`. The even square-zero direction raises the tangent dimension to `3`. At the even global identity the tangent dimension is `2`; at the odd global identity it is `1`.

### Proposition 9.3 — finite-field points and zeta functions

Let `q` be a power of two. For every `n\ge1`,

\[
\#C(\mathbf F_{q^n})
=
\#M_E(\mathbf F_{q^n})
=2q^n-1,
\]

and

\[
\#M_O(\mathbf F_{q^n})=3q^n-2.
\]

Therefore

\[
Z(C,T)=Z(M_E,T)=\frac{1-T}{(1-qT)^2},
\]

\[
Z(M_O,T)=\frac{(1-T)^2}{(1-qT)^3}.
\]

The equality for `M_E` and its reduction is the expected invisibility of nilpotent thickening to field-valued points, not a separate arithmetic anomaly.

### Proposition 9.4 — finite Artin counts

For

\[
R_n=\mathbf F_2[t]/(t^n),
\]

\[
|M_E(R_n)|
=(n+2)2^{n-1+\lfloor n/2\rfloor},
\]

\[
|M_O(R_n)|
=(n+3)2^{n-1}.
\]

The idempotent counts are

\[
|\operatorname{Idem}(M_E)(R_n)|
=2+2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor},
\qquad
|\operatorname{Idem}(M_O)(R_n)|=4.
\]

#### Proof

The crossing has `(n+2)2^{n-1}` points. The square-zero elements of `R_n` are the multiples of `t^{\lceil n/2\rceil}`, so there are `2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}` choices for `\eta`. For the odd monoid, the local ring `R_n` has only the idempotents zero and one, so the clopen scheme decomposition becomes a genuine disjoint sum of the unit and crossing point sets. ∎
