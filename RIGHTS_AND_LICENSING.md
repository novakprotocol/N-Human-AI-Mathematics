# Rights and Licensing

## Default

Unless a file or release states otherwise:

```text
Copyright © 2026 Matthew S. Novak. All rights reserved.
```

No blanket MIT license applies to this repository.

## Why rights are separated

A mathematical publication package contains different kinds of material:

- manuscript prose and equations;
- figures and teaching material;
- source code;
- formal proof source;
- machine-readable data and evidence;
- third-party quotations, references, or adapted material.

One software license should not silently govern all of them.

## Required notices

Each paper package must declare:

```text
MANUSCRIPT_TERMS.md
CODE_TERMS.md
DATA_AND_EVIDENCE_TERMS.md
THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md
```

A release may later adopt explicit open terms, but only through an owner-approved change tied to an exact version.

## Contributions

Human contributors must confirm that they have the right to submit their material and identify any incompatible terms. The repository does not accept unverified copied proofs, proprietary datasets, or figures scraped from publications.

## References and quotations

Bibliographic facts are not licensed content, but copied prose, figures, and extensive quotations may be protected. Prefer original exposition and short, properly attributed quotations where necessary.

## AI-generated material

AI systems cannot grant rights. The accountable human author must review AI-assisted text, code, and figures for originality, accuracy, and third-party contamination before release.

## Formal libraries

Formal proof files may depend on libraries with their own licenses. Preserve those notices and do not imply that the repository owns external library code.

## Public review

Public visibility grants access to read the repository. It does not, by itself, grant permission to republish, sell, sublicense, or incorporate protected material beyond applicable law and the file-specific terms.

## Future open release

Before adopting any open license, the owner should decide separately for:

1. manuscripts;
2. code;
3. formal proofs;
4. data/evidence;
5. figures and teaching assets.

The decision should consider journal requirements, DOI archival, contributor rights, patent or disclosure concerns, and the desired level of downstream reuse.
